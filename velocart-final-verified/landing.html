01 / FOUNDATION
THE BIG PICTURE

What is Ecommerce
Business Intelligence?

Ecommerce business intelligence (BI) uses store data to understand performance, uncover what drives results, and decide what to improve next.

⌁

One connected view of your business

Instead of jumping between store reports, advertising platforms, spreadsheets, and inventory tools, BI brings information together into a clear view of sales, marketing, customers, and operations.

With consistent reporting, teams can see which products generate revenue, which channels are profitable, where customers drop off, and how stock affects sales. The aim is simple: better decisions based on performance rather than assumptions.

03 / MEASUREMENT
MEASURE WHAT MATTERS

Key ecommerce KPIs
to track.

Start with a small set of clear, consistently defined metrics. Read them together to understand not only how much you sell, but how efficiently and sustainably you grow.

₹

Revenue & AOV

Revenue shows sales generated over a period. Average order value (AOV) reveals how much customers spend per transaction and helps assess pricing or bundles.

SALES PERFORMANCE
↗

Conversion & acquisition

Conversion rate measures the share of visitors who buy. Customer acquisition cost (CAC) and return on ad spend (ROAS) help evaluate marketing efficiency.

MARKETING EFFICIENCY
♡

Lifetime value & retention

Customer lifetime value (LTV) estimates customer value over time. Compare it with CAC to guide acquisition and retention investments.

CUSTOMER VALUE
▦

Inventory velocity

Stock turnover and sell-through rate show how efficiently products move, helping teams reduce overstock and avoid missed sales from low stock.

OPERATIONS
✦

Tip: Agree on definitions before comparing reports. Revenue, returns, discounts, and reporting dates should be calculated consistently across your data sources.

04 / IN PRACTICE
FROM DATA TO ACTION

9 key applications of
ecommerce BI.

BI supports decisions across the full online-store journey — from product demand and campaign efficiency to customer loyalty and fulfilment.

01

Ecommerce performance analytics

Track revenue trends by day, week, and month; compare products, variants, regions, and discount effectiveness.

02

Sales analytics

Review sales volume and the purchase journey to find high-performing channels, landing pages, and customer segments.

03

Marketing analytics

Connect advertising spend with revenue and cost of goods to assess whether campaigns produce profitable growth.

04

Customer analytics & segmentation

Understand new, active, lapsed, and dormant customers so messaging can better match customer needs.

05

Lifetime value & retention

Compare new and returning customers, purchase frequency, and cohorts to learn how customer value changes over time.

06

SEO analytics

Measure organic sessions, engagement, landing-page performance, and which pages help turn search traffic into sales.

07

Supply chain & inventory

Compare incoming stock, sales velocity, and remaining inventory to identify overstock, understock, and slow movers.

08

Operational performance

Monitor order processing, fulfilment, delivery, and returns to spot bottlenecks and protect customer experience.

09

Competitor & experience analytics

Review pricing, promotions, site speed, and checkout friction to identify opportunities to improve conversion and retention.

06 / IMPLEMENTATION
BUILD WITH PURPOSE

Common challenges.
Practical habits.

Useful BI is not about adding the most charts. It is about reliable data, understandable metrics, and reports connected to real decisions.

!

Common challenges

  • Store, advertising, and analytics data live in separate tools.
  • Revenue, AOV, and LTV are defined differently across reports.
  • Manual exports become outdated quickly.
  • Metrics have no clear owner across teams.
  • Dashboards become overloaded and rarely used.
✓

Best practices

  • Start with 5–8 core KPIs instead of dozens of charts.
  • Standardise column names, dates, and calculation rules early.
  • Keep admin analysis and customer-facing views distinct.
  • Review slow-moving and low-stock products regularly.
  • Connect every dashboard to a decision and an owner.
